✦ Fun Fact

One small thing worth knowing

Mantis shrimp have 16 types of colour-photoreceptor cells — compared to just three in human eyes — yet laboratory tests show they are surprisingly poor at distinguishing similar shades. Scientists now believe they “sample” colour at each point in space rather than comparing hues across the field of view, trading fine colour discrimination for near-instant processing speed.

— Royal Society Biology Letters (Thoen et al., 2014); also confirmed in Smithsonian Magazine

🧩 Puzzle

Two minutes of thinking

Type: Logic

Three boxes are mislabelled. Box 1 says “Apples,” Box 2 says “Oranges,” Box 3 says “Apples and Oranges.” All three labels are wrong — none of the boxes actually contains what its label says. You may pick exactly one fruit from exactly one box, without looking inside first. Which box do you pick from, and how does that single pick let you correctly label all three boxes?

How to approach: focus on the most constrained box — if all labels are wrong, what are the only possible contents of the “Apples and Oranges” box?

Show answer

Pick from Box 3 (labelled “Apples and Oranges”). Since all labels are wrong, this box cannot be mixed — it holds either only apples or only oranges. Case 1: you draw an apple. Box 3 is the all-Apples box. Box 1 (labelled “Apples”) cannot be Apples (taken by Box 3); if it were Mixed, Box 2 would have to be Oranges, but Box 2’s label also says “Oranges” — that would make one label correct, contradicting the premise. So Box 1 must be Oranges and Box 2 must be Mixed. Case 2: you draw an orange. Box 3 is Oranges. By the same logic, Box 2 must be Apples and Box 1 must be Mixed.
